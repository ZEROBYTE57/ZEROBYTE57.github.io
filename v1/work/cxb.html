Connectome Explanation Benchmark (CXB)

Do brain-network explanations find the disease or the scanner?

A benchmark that plants a known connectivity effect into real ABIDE I fMRI connectomes (848 subjects, 19 hospitals), so model explanations can be scored against an exact ground truth.

The problem

Graph neural networks on brain connectomes are often explained with saliency maps that highlight the connections a model relied on. With real patients, though, nobody knows which connections truly carry the disease, so there is no way to tell whether an explanation points at the disease or at something else, such as which hospital scanner recorded the data.

Multi-site datasets like ABIDE mix 19 hospitals, and scanner differences can quietly become a shortcut the model learns instead of the disease.

The approach

CXB makes the correct answer known:

  1. Planted ground truth. A known connectivity effect is planted into real healthy-control connectomes from ABIDE, so the "disease" connections are known exactly. The signal is added to the time series of 10 regions and connectivity is recomputed, so every matrix stays a valid correlation matrix.
  2. Scanner shortcut. A scanner effect, sized from real differences between ABIDE hospitals, is added to the planted scans and linked to the label during training only.
  3. Scoring explanations. Explanations are scored on whether they point at the planted effect or at the scanner. Fixes are compared: four ComBat harmonisation versions and counterfactual site training (CF-Site), where each training scan is paired with its counterfactual at another scanner.

Set-up

DataABIDE I (Preprocessed Connectomes Project, CPAC, no global signal regression), AAL-116 atlas, 848 subjects from 19 hospitals
ModelsLogistic regression, MLP, GCN (BrainGB set-up), BrainNetCNN; BrainGNN and Brain Network Transformer reimplemented and reported where they fail a sanity test
ExplainersSaliency, Integrated Gradients, a GNNExplainer-style input mask
MetricsDisease AUPRC on the planted connections, scanner share of the explanation, test accuracy
EvaluationLeave-site-out folds, model selection on validation data only, split-level confidence intervals, paired tests with Holm correction

Results

  • Accuracy hides the scanner shortcut; the explanation shows it. As the scanner–label link in training grows from 50% to 90%, the scanner share of the top explanation connections rises by 0.43 (MLP) and 0.22 (GCN), while test accuracy falls by only 0.03 (MLP) and 0.07 (GCN), over 10 splits × 3 seeds.
  • The ComBat version decides the outcome. Under the shortcut, GCN disease recovery ranges from 0.02 to 0.63 across four common ComBat versions.
  • CF-Site beats plain training by +0.15 AUPRC and matches the best ComBat without harmonising new-site data. With the true other-scanner scan (oracle CF-Site), recovery reaches 0.85–0.90: the limit is the scanner model, not the training method.

What it shows

A model can look fine on accuracy while its explanations quietly drift to the scanner. Benchmarks with a planted, known answer make that drift measurable, and they show that the choice of harmonisation matters as much as the model.
